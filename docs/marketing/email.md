Emailing attendees and potential attendees has multiple important parts to be aware of. There are a few different ways to do this:

## Ti.to 

Good for emailing ticketholders and prior year ticketholders, easy to segment by ticket type. Markdown for formatting. If needed, email addresses can be manually excluded and included.

- Welcome emails 
- Reminders to assign tickets
- Sprint attendee information

## Pretalx

Good for emailing speakers, CFP applicants, etc.

## Email Octopus

Ideally for email marketing only. Think: Cold email outreach, promotions, etc. There are laws around email marketing specifically, please read up on them:


- [CAN-SPAM Best Practices for Email Marketing](https://help.myemma.com/s/article/How-Can-Spam-affects-your-email-marketing)
- [CAN-SPAM Act and how it affects your campaigns](https://knowledgebase.constantcontact.com/email-digital-marketing/articles/KnowledgeBase/5825-CAN-SPAM-Act-and-how-it-affects-your-campaigns?lang=en_US)

### Contacts

It's important we keep our email list well organized. Any time emails get imported, add the appropriate tag for ticket type, AND by year. For example, in-person ticketed attendees of 2026: [in-person], [ticketed2026]. In the meantime, contacts must be copied over from ti.to via CSV.

### Campaigns

A given email can have multiple campaigns, for example an "Early bird tickets end soon" email can be tailored to specific audiences with their own email content and own recipients, for example:

- Online: [online] tag, content tailored to the $99 ticket
- IRL: [ticketed2025], [ticketed2026] tags, content tailored to the city itself and IRL events

Don't take this as gospel, take some time to tailor campaigns that will deliver the biggest punch.
